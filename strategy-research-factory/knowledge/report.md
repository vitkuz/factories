# The report

An executive-quality consulting report, written from the approved storyline. It is written for a
decision maker who has five minutes and may go deeper, and for a reviewer who will check every
number. The writer does not search. A fact missing from the evidence base is a known gap, and the
report says so.

## Structure, in this order

1. **Title.** `# Main phrase: subtitle`, then an italic one-sentence dek with the headline number.
   Then one line: audience, geography, timeframe, and the date the data is as of.
2. **At a glance.** Exactly five bullets. Each opens with a **bold one-sentence claim**, followed
   by one or two sentences with the number and the mechanism. At least four carry a number or a
   range. The last bullet is the implication or call to action.
3. **Executive summary.** The governing thought first, then one paragraph per key message, each
   opening with a **bold lead sentence**. Recommendations in brief. One to two pages. It must
   stand alone.
4. **Context.** Situation, complication, the decision and the question. Scope and what is out of
   scope. A roadmap of the chapters.
5. **Key findings.** One `## <n>. <claim heading>` chapter per key message, with `### <claim>`
   sections for the supporting points. Framework results appear where the storyline puts them,
   as a compact table or list with its evidence, introduced by what it shows.
6. **Strategic implications.** What the findings mean for the decision maker: the so-what,
   gathered.
7. **Recommendations.** Each has a bold imperative, why it follows (the chapter it comes from),
   an impact range, the first step, an owner and timing. Then **key questions for leaders**:
   four to six.
8. **Risks and uncertainties.** The conditions that must hold, the strongest counter-argument,
   leading indicators to watch, and any storyline-review finding still open.
9. **Methodology.** Decision-first framing, the issue tree (top two levels), the frameworks used
   and **why**, including branches that used a custom analysis instead and frameworks considered
   but rejected. The hypotheses tested, as a table of id, statement, status, confidence and
   decisive evidence ids. The research lenses. How derived numbers were computed. Known gaps. A
   statement that this is desk research from public sources, not a forecast.
10. **Sources.** A numbered list, `[EV-012] Publisher, "Title", date. URL (tier)`. List only the
    sources the report cites.
11. **Appendix.** Framework outputs in full where they were compacted above, and findings that
    did not reach an implication.

## Writing rules

- **Answer first**, in every chapter, section and paragraph.
- **Headings are claims.** Bad: "Market size". Good: "The market could exceed $20B by 2030,
  driven primarily by enterprise infrastructure spending".
- **Cite as you go.** Every factual sentence ends with its evidence ids `(EV-012)`. Derived
  figures cite their inputs and say "we estimate".
- **Label honestly.** Inference is never written as fact. An untested or insufficient hypothesis
  is never written as a finding.
- **Numbers, not adjectives.** Every number is in the number register, with the same value, unit
  and year everywhere.
- **Calibrated.** Forward numbers are ranges or scenarios. Use the likelihood words from the
  methodology.
- Short sentences, active voice, plain words. Define acronyms once.
- Length follows the evidence, typically 3,000–6,000 words.

## On a revision pass

Fix exactly the findings assigned to the report and leave everything else untouched. Open the
file with a `## Review points addressed` section that lists the finding ids fixed and how. The
package step removes that section.
