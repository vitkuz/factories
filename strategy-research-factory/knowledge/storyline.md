# The storyline

The storyline is the report as a skeleton: every heading, in order, with the evidence under each.
It is reviewed before a word of prose exists, because good writing cannot rescue a bad storyline.
The form is Minto's Pyramid Principle.

## Pyramid rules

1. Every idea summarises the ideas grouped below it.
2. Ideas in a group are the same kind (all reasons, all steps, all segments).
3. Each group follows a stated logical order: time, structure or importance.
4. Groups hold three to five ideas.
5. **Vertical logic:** each statement raises a question (why? how? so what?), and the level below
   answers exactly that question.
6. **Horizontal logic:** prefer an **inductive** key line (parallel reasons, each supporting the
   answer, so that one failed point leaves the others standing). Use a deductive chain only when
   the logic truly is a chain.

## Shape — `storyline.md`

1. **SCQA.** Situation, complication, question (the problem statement), answer (the governing
   thought).
2. **Title and dek.** "Main phrase: subtitle", plus one sentence carrying the headline number.
3. **At a glance (draft).** Five bullets. Each opens with a one-sentence claim, and at least four
   carry a number or range. The last bullet is the implication or call to action.
4. **The pyramid:**

   ```
   MAIN ANSWER
   <governing thought>
     1. <key message: full-sentence action title>      [insights I1, I2]
        – <supporting point>  (EV-..., register key)
        – <supporting point>
     2. <key message> …
     …
     N. What this means for <decision maker>           [REC1..]
   ```

5. **Frameworks in the story.** Say where each framework result appears (which message) and how
   it is used. A framework result that no message needs is dropped from the story, not forced in.
6. **Titles-only test.** Every heading alone, in order. Say whether they tell the whole argument
   and land on the recommendation.
7. **Open limitations.** What the story cannot claim, and why.

## Rules

- Every heading is a claim with a subject and a verb, usually with a number. "The market could
  exceed $20B by 2030, driven by infrastructure spend", not "Market size".
- Every supporting point cites evidence ids or register keys. A point with none is cut.
- Only the synthesis's insights appear. The storyline organises. It does not discover.
- Frameworks never become the chapter structure. The chapters are the key messages.
- On a revision pass: fix what the review lists and keep the rest. Start with a short list of the
  review points addressed.
