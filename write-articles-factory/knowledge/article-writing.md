# Writing an article from research

## Inputs you get
- The research notes and the insights file: the only facts you may use.
- The selection file: which angle is yours. Write that angle only — do not
  re-pick, merge angles, or drift into another article's territory.

## Shape
- A working title, a one-sentence dek, then the body in Markdown.
- Open on the angle's hook — a concrete scene, number, or contradiction — never
  on a definition or "In today's world".
- One idea per section; H2 headings that say something, not "Introduction".
- End on a consequence or a question the reader keeps, not a summary.
- Target the length you are given, within ±15%.

## Facts
- Every factual claim (number, date, name, quote, study) carries an inline
  source marker `[n]`, and a `## Sources` list at the end maps `[n]` to a URL
  from the research notes.
- No claim that is not in the research. If you need it and it is not there,
  cut the sentence rather than invent support.
- Quotes are verbatim from a source or not quotes.

## Revision pass
When you are given a fact-check report, fix exactly the issues it lists for
your article — correct, source or remove each claim — and leave the rest of
the text alone.
