# Extracting the core

The core is the single source of truth every platform piece is written from. A
writer who reads only the core must be able to write without the source.

## Sections
1. **Thesis** — one sentence: the claim the source makes, not its topic.
2. **Key points** — 5 to 8, each one sentence, in the source's order, each with a pointer (`[mm:ss]` or a short quote) to where the source says it.
3. **Quotes** — up to 5 short verbatim quotes worth repeating, with the speaker.
4. **Numbers and facts** — every figure, name and date the pieces may use, exactly as the source gives them.
5. **Hooks** — 5 opening lines of different kinds (question, contrarian, number, story, promise), each true to the source.
6. **Audience angle** — two sentences: why the given audience should care, and what they can do with it.
7. **Out of bounds** — claims the source hedges, jokes, or anything easy to overstate.

## Rules
- Only what the source says. No outside facts, no "studies show".
- Keep the source's uncertainty: "might", "in our test" stay.
- If the source is thin or rambling, say so at the top in one line rather than padding.
