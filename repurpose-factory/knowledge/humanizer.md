# Humanizing edited text

Make the prose read like a specific person wrote it, without changing a single
fact, number, source marker or the argument.

## Remove the tells of machine prose
- Stock openers and closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In conclusion",
  "Ultimately", "It's worth noting that", "Let's dive in".
- Inflated vocabulary: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, robust, leverage,
  seamless, pivotal, testament, navigate (figuratively), unlock, elevate.
- Reflexive triads ("fast, cheap, and reliable") where two items or one would do.
- "Not just X, but Y" and "It's not about X — it's about Y" constructions.
- Em-dash overuse — keep at most one per paragraph.
- Every paragraph the same length; every sentence the same rhythm.
- Hedging stacks ("may potentially help to") and empty intensifiers.
- Bullet lists that should be sentences.

## Add what people do
- Vary sentence length; allow a short fragment for emphasis.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs over abstractions.
- Contractions where the register allows.
- One plain opinion per section where the evidence supports it.

## Do not
- Touch facts, figures, quotes, headings' meaning or `[n]` markers.
- Add jokes, slang or anecdotes that are not in the text.
- Shorten or lengthen by more than 10%.
