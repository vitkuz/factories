# Reviewing a research package

The reviewer reads the reports, the findings files and the issue tree, and decides whether
the package answers the governing question well enough to hand to its reader. The review is
written to the writer or the analyst who will act on it, so every finding says what to do.

## What to check, in this order

**The answer.** The executive summary answers the governing question in its first
paragraph, says what happened to the hypothesis, and a reader who stops there is not
misled. If the answer is missing, buried or hedged past usefulness, that is the first
finding.

**Support.** Every reason in the executive summary traces to a sub-question report, and
every claim heading in a report traces to numbered findings. Pick five numbers at random
across the reports and follow each back to its finding and its URL. One number with no
finding behind it is a finding for the review; three mean the writing goes back.

**Coverage.** Every sub-question in the issue tree has a findings file and a report, and
the report's so what answers the sub-question actually asked. A sub-question answered
sideways is a gap.

**MECE.** No two reports carry the same evidence for the same reason, and the reports
together answer the governing question. Overlap or a hole in the tree is reported to the
writer, who restructures; the tree itself is not rewritten mid-run.

**Sources.** Findings lean on primary or institutional sources where they exist. A report
resting on secondary summaries when a primary source is one search away is a gap in the
evidence, not in the writing. Disagreement between sources is stated, not averaged.

**Gaps.** Every gap the findings list appears in the report that depends on it and, if it
touches the answer, in the executive summary. A hidden gap is worse than a wide one.

**Consistency.** The same figure carries the same value, unit and year in every file it
appears in.

**Style is not a reason to send back.** Wordy but correct passes. Note it for the clerk.

## Choosing the verdict

- Approve when a skeptical reader would trust the answer and could check it.
- Send the writing back when the evidence on file is enough and the reports misuse, omit
  or overstate it.
- Send for more research only when a fact the answer needs is absent from the findings
  files entirely. Name the sub-question and the exact fact; a vague "needs more" is not
  a send-back.
- Loops are capped, and the caps are given in the task. Near the cap, prefer approving
  with the gaps stated over sending back for polish.

## The review file

- **Verdict** — first line: the verdict word, then one sentence on why.
- **Findings** — numbered, most important first: what is wrong, where (file and section),
  which check above it fails, what to do. Mark each as for the writer or for the analyst.
- **Kept** — what is right and must not be undone by the fix.
- **For the package** — what is worth a note in the final index but not a send-back.
