# How a research report is shaped

A research run produces one report per sub-question plus an executive summary. They are
written for a reader with five minutes who will act on them, and for a reviewer who will
check every claim against the findings files. The findings files are the only source; the
writer adds structure and judgement, never facts.

## The executive summary

Named `00-executive-summary` so it sorts first. It stands alone: a reader who opens nothing
else gets the answer.

1. **Answer** — the first paragraph answers the governing question in two to four
   sentences. It says whether the initial hypothesis held, failed or partly held.
2. **Why** — three to five bullet points, one per supporting reason, each ending with the
   sub-question number it draws on. These are the "so whats" of the sub-question reports.
3. **What it would take to be wrong** — two or three lines on the evidence that, if it
   appeared, would change the answer.
4. **Known gaps** — every gap the findings files list that touches the answer, one line
   each, with what it would cost to close it.
5. **Reading order** — the sub-question reports in the order a reader should take them, one
   line each with the report's so what.

## A sub-question report

Named `<nn>-<slug>` from the issue tree, so it sits beside its findings file.

1. **So what** — one sentence, the answer to the sub-question, as the first line under the
   title. Then one paragraph on why it matters to the governing question.
2. **Verdict on the hypothesis** — held, failed or partly held, in one line.
3. **The evidence** — three to five sections. Each heading is a claim, not a label
   ("Running costs fall by 30% per km", not "Costs"). Under each heading, the findings that
   support it by number (`F3`, `F7`) with the number, unit and date restated, and a source
   line per finding: outlet and URL.
4. **Where sources disagree** — the disagreement section of the findings file, resolved by
   the writer with a reason, or left open and said to be open.
5. **Gaps** — copied from the findings file, unchanged.

## Rules

- Answer first, always. No section builds up to its point.
- Every number carries its finding number and its source URL. A number with no finding
  behind it is removed, not hedged.
- The writer does not search. A fact missing from the findings files goes under gaps, and
  the report says so; the reviewer decides whether to send for more research.
- Short sentences, active voice, no adjectives that do the work a number should do.
  "Large" is out; "twelve percent" is in.
- Numbers are consistent across reports: the same figure appears with the same value, unit
  and year everywhere.
- On a revision pass, when a review lists what to change, change exactly that and leave
  everything else untouched. The executive summary opens with a short block naming the
  review points addressed, which the clerk removes in the final package.
