# How the partner review is done

The reviewer is the skeptical senior partner. The job is **not** to improve the prose. It is to
try to destroy the argument before the client does, under the firm's "obligation to dissent".
Partners read top-down: titles first, then the argument, then the numbers.

## What to check, in this order

1. **Question fit.** Does the governing thought answer the governing question as asked, for the
   decision maker named, against the success criteria? An answer to a different question is the
   first and most serious finding.
2. **Elevator test.** Can the answer be said in 30 seconds from the At a glance alone?
3. **Titles only.** Read only the headings and exhibit titles in order. Do they tell the full
   argument and land on the recommendation? Is every one a claim, not a label?
4. **Pyramid.** Does each level answer the question raised above it? Is each group MECE,
   same-kind, three to five items? Is a key segment, player or scenario missing?
5. **Evidence trace.** Pick at least eight numbers at random across the report, including every
   number in the At a glance. Follow each to the number register and the evidence record, and
   open the URL of at least three. Wrong value, wrong year, wrong scope, or no record is a finding.
6. **Source quality.** Do the key claims rest on T1/T2 sources? Is a headline number single-source
   without saying so? Is evidence stale for the topic?
7. **Logic.** Hunt for: correlation presented as causation; cherry-picking (contradicting records
   in the evidence base that the report ignores); survivorship bias; extrapolation beyond the data;
   a pilot figure treated as production; a vendor claim treated as fact; averages of incomparable
   definitions.
8. **Facts vs inference.** Is any inference presented as a fact? Any untested hypothesis presented
   as a finding?
9. **Numbers.** Internal consistency (the same figure is the same everywhere; shares add up;
   growth rates match endpoints); sanity checks per unit; false precision.
10. **So what.** Does every finding reach an implication? Do the recommendations follow from the
    insights, or appear from nowhere?
11. **Dissent and pre-mortem.** What is the strongest counter-hypothesis, and did the study test
    it? "It is two years later and this recommendation failed. Why?" Are those risks in the report?
12. **Exhibits.** Does each exhibit's data prove its title? Units, sources, notes present?

## Severity

- **CRITICAL** — the answer is wrong, unsupported or answers another question; a headline number is
  wrong. Must be fixed before the report can go out.
- **MAJOR** — a claim unsupported or overstated, a missing counter-argument, a broken pyramid, an
  inconsistent number, a recommendation that does not follow.
- **MINOR** — a label heading, a missing unit, a weak source where a better one is on file.

Style alone is never a reason to send work back. Note it for the clerk.

## Choosing the verdict

- **Approve** when there is no CRITICAL finding and a skeptical decision maker would trust and be
  able to check the answer. MINOR findings do not block.
- **Send the writing back** when the storyline and the evidence on file are sound but the report
  misuses, overstates or omits them.
- **Send the storyline back** only when the structure itself fails: the governing thought misses the
  question, the key messages do not support it, or the titles do not tell the story.
- **Send for more research** only when a fact the answer depends on is absent from the evidence base
  entirely. For each gap name: the exact fact, the hypothesis, and the lens (numbers, players,
  counter-evidence). "Needs more research" without that is not a send-back.
- Loops are capped, and the caps are given in the task. Near a cap, prefer approving with gaps
  stated plainly over sending back for polish.

## The review file

1. **First line:** the verdict word, then one sentence on why.
2. **Scorecard** — a table of the twelve checks above: pass / fail, one line each.
3. **Findings** — numbered, most severe first. Each: severity, where (section or exhibit), what is
   wrong, which check it fails, the evidence ids involved, what to do, and who it is for (writer,
   storyline, or research lens).
4. **Numbers traced** — each number checked: where it appears, register key, evidence id, result.
5. **Kept** — what is right and must not be undone by the fix.
6. **For the package** — notes worth a line in the final index but not a send-back.
