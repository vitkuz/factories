# Synthesis

Synthesis is the hardest step. You have to step back from the detail and separate what is important from what is merely interesting (McKinsey staff paper).
The output is a small number of insights that change the decision, plus a recommendation someone can act on.

## Synthesis vs summary
- **Summary** restates what the dossiers say, lens by lens. "The market is EUR 400M; there are 5 competitors."
- **Synthesis** combines evidence across lenses to answer the key question. "The segment is big enough, but incumbents' switching costs cap realistic share at 3–5%, so the business case works only with a partner channel."
- Test: if a sentence could be pasted into a report on a different question, it is summary, not synthesis.

## The "so what?" ladder
Push every finding up four rungs. Stop only when you reach an action, or when you can show the finding doesn't matter.

| Rung | Question | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| Finding | What did we observe? | 62% of target SMBs use a free bundled tool. |
| Insight | Why does it matter / what does it reveal? | Our real competitor is "free and good enough", not the paid rivals. |
| Implication | What does it mean for the decision, quantified? | Paid conversion likely 2–4%, not 8%; SOM falls to EUR 6–12M. |
| Action | What should the decision-maker do? | Lead with a freemium tier; re-test the price after 6 months. |

Write every rung as a declarative sentence, not a topic. "Pricing" is a topic. "Pricing must drop 20% to beat substitutes" is an insight. If the decision-maker's first reaction would be "so what should we do?", you haven't finished.

## Test every hypothesis
For each hypothesis in the problem frame, give a verdict against its pre-stated confirm and kill criteria:
- **Confirmed:** confirm criteria met, no graded evidence refutes it.
- **Revised:** partly true. Restate it precisely (scope, size, timing) and say what changed.
- **Killed:** kill criteria met. Say what the evidence shows instead.
- **Unresolved:** evidence is insufficient. Name the gap and how much it matters.

Cite the evidence IDs and grades behind each verdict. A verdict that rests only on single-source, stale or assumption-grade evidence is at most "leaning", and you must say so.
Also ask: "What would happen if all the hypotheses came true?" That test can surface second-order problems such as regulatory or competitive backlash.

## Pick the 3–5 insights that change the decision
- Rank insights by how much they would move the decision or the value at stake, not by how interesting they are.
- Keep an insight only if removing it would change the recommendation, its size, or its risk profile.
- Group what is left MECE under one governing thought, which is the answer to the key question.
- Leave interesting-but-irrelevant findings out, or put them in an appendix.

## Quantify with ranges
- Give every implication a **low / base / high** range and name the driver behind the spread.
- Show the arithmetic and the evidence IDs for every number.
- Round to the precision the evidence supports. "EUR 8–14M", not "EUR 11.37M".
- Name the 1–3 assumptions that the outcome depends on most (from the sensitivity analysis), and the breakeven values.

## Options, trade-offs, uncertainty
- Consider at least 2 real alternatives besides the recommendation, including "do nothing / wait" when it is credible. Evaluate them on the same success criteria.
- Make trade-offs explicit, e.g. speed vs cost, share vs margin, control vs capital.
- For each key uncertainty, say whether it is **reducible** (more research, a pilot) or **irreducible** (hedge it, stage the investment, keep options open).
- Express likelihood in consistent words, and add a numeric range when possible (see the executive writing guidance).

## Recommendations
Each recommendation must be:
- **Specific:** what, where, how much.
- **Owned:** a named role.
- **Sequenced:** first moves and dependencies, including quick wins.
- **Risk-aware:** the main risks, each with a mitigation.
- **Measurable:** **leading indicators** that show within weeks or months whether it is working, with a threshold that triggers a rethink.

## Template: synthesis

```
# Synthesis: <title>
Key question: <copied from the problem frame>
Governing thought (answer): <one sentence>

## Hypothesis verdicts
| H | Statement | Verdict | Evidence (IDs, grades) | Note |
|---|-----------|---------|------------------------|------|

## Key insights (3–5, ranked)
1. <insight sentence>
   - Finding → insight → implication (range) → action
   - Evidence: E3 (A1), E8 (B2) · confidence: high/med/low

## Quantified implications
| Metric | Low | Base | High | Driver of spread | Evidence |

## Options considered
| Option | Fit to success criteria | Value (range) | Risk | Why chosen / rejected |

## Key uncertainties
U1 <uncertainty> · reducible? · effect on decision · how to reduce or hedge

## Deliberately excluded
<interesting findings left out and why>
```

## Template: recommendations

```
# Recommendations: <title>
Recommendation: <one sentence, action + scale + timing>

| # | Action | Owner (role) | When / sequence | Depends on | Cost / resources |
|---|--------|--------------|-----------------|------------|------------------|

## Risks & mitigations
| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Early warning sign |

## Leading indicators
| Indicator | Target | Check date | If missed → |

## Decision gates / conditions to revisit
<what evidence would change this recommendation>
```
