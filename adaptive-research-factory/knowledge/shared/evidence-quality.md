# Evidence Quality

Evidence is graded before it is used. The grade decides what the evidence can carry.
Grade each item on its own. Then grade the set. Then decide the hypothesis status.

## 1. Classify every claim

- FACT: verifiable from a named source. Observed or reported data.
- ESTIMATE: a number derived by a stated method. Show inputs and the calculation.
- ASSUMPTION: accepted to proceed, not verified. Flag it if the conclusion depends on it.
- OPINION: a judgment by a person or organization. Attribute it to its author.

An opinion repeated ten times is still an opinion.
Never silently convert weak evidence into fact. If a claim is weak, keep its label and its grade visible.

## 2. Grade each evidence item: HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW

Check five things: source type, independence, recency, directness, sample size.

HIGH needs all of these:
- primary or official source (statistics office, regulator, filing, transaction register, product docs)
- the exact claim appears in the source
- data date within the freshness window for the topic
- measures the thing in the hypothesis, not a proxy
- not a single anecdote

MEDIUM is any of these, with no LOW trigger:
- credible secondary source (research firm with stated method, major press, named expert)
- primary data, but a few months stale or a slightly different scope
- a good proxy with a stated gap
- a small but real sample

LOW is any one of these:
- unsourced, anonymous, or promotional content
- a secondary figure you cannot trace to a primary source
- old data on a fast-moving topic
- a weak proxy
- one anecdote, one listing, one review
- model memory with no source

When two criteria point different ways, take the lower grade.

## 3. Independence

Count sources, not pages.
- Two pages quoting one press release are one source.
- Several articles citing the same report are one source.
- A site that copies a listing from a portal is the same source as the portal.
- Independent means: separate origin of the data, collected separately.

Trace each important secondary figure to its origin. If you cannot, grade it LOW.
Record the origin next to the claim so the count can be checked.

## 4. Recency

Record two dates: when the data describes, and when it was published.
- Prices, market sizes, competitor facts, listings: stale after about 12 to 24 months.
- Laws and regulations: only the current version counts.
- Slow-changing facts (geography, building age) may be older.
- Stale evidence is capped at MEDIUM, and at LOW if the topic is fast-moving.

## 5. Directness

Ask: does this measure the thing the hypothesis names?
- Direct: actual sale prices for the hypothesis about resale prices.
- Proxy: asking prices, a neighboring district, a national average, a forecast.
- A proxy can support a hypothesis. It cannot confirm it alone.
- Name the proxy and the gap in the limitations field.

## 6. Sample size

- One data point is an example, not a pattern.
- State the count: "6 comparable listings", not "many listings".
- Check that the sample is not selected: listings still online are not all listings.
- A small sample caps the item at MEDIUM. A single case caps it at LOW.

## 7. What a set of evidence can carry

Count independent items only. Tag each item supports, refutes, or neutral.

- SUPPORTED: at least 2 independent HIGH items support it, or 1 HIGH plus 2 MEDIUM. No HIGH or MEDIUM item refutes it.
- PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED: the evidence supports part of the claim or holds under conditions. Or support is MEDIUM only. Or HIGH support is met by credible refuting items. State which part holds.
- REJECTED: at least 1 HIGH item, or 2 independent MEDIUM items, directly refute it. Support, if any, is weaker.
- INCONCLUSIVE: evidence is missing, LOW only, a single source, or balanced and unresolved. Say what would resolve it.

Rules for the call:
- LOW items never decide a status alone. They may suggest where to look.
- Absence of evidence is not refutation. Searching and finding nothing is INCONCLUSIVE, unless the data should exist and does not.
- If grades and counts disagree, choose the weaker status.
- Set the status confidence by the weakest item the verdict depends on.

## 8. Separate facts, interpretation, uncertainty

Write three blocks for every evaluation.
- Facts: graded items with source and date. Nothing else.
- Interpretation: what the facts mean together. Mark it as judgment.
- Uncertainty: gaps, proxies, stale items, conflicts, and what could change the verdict.

Keep contradicting evidence. List it. Do not drop or average it away.
If sources disagree, record both, explain the gap (definition, year, scope), and choose a working value with a reason.

## 9. Worked example

Hypothesis: a $55k studio in Didi Dighomi can be resold within 6 months without a large discount.

Evidence:
- E1: 40 comparable listings, asking prices only, from one portal. Directness is weak (asking, not sold). MEDIUM.
- E2: two news pages about "slow resales", both quoting one agency press release. One source, untraced to data. LOW.
- E3: statistics office district sales volume, 14 months old. Official, but old and district-wide. MEDIUM.
- E4: one broker's opinion that studios sell fast. OPINION, LOW.

Result:
- No HIGH item. Support is MEDIUM at best and indirect.
- Status: INCONCLUSIVE, leaning partially supported. Not SUPPORTED.
- Facts: E1 count and price spread, E3 volume. Interpretation: listings exist, but speed of sale is unknown. Uncertainty: no sold-price or days-on-market data.
- To close the gap: transaction records or three broker confirmations.
